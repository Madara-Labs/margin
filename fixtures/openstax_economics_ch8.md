7 Cost and Industry Structure

7.1 Explicit and Implicit Costs, and Accounting and Economic Profit

Every firm strives to earn a profit, which is the difference between total revenue and total cost:

$$\Pi = TR - TC$$ (7.1)

where $TR = P \times Q$ represents total revenue from selling quantity $Q$ at market price $P$, and $TC$ represents total cost.

Accounting profit is a cash concept. It means total revenue minus explicit costs—the difference between dollars brought in and dollars paid out. Economic profit is total revenue minus total cost, including both explicit and implicit opportunity costs:

$$\Pi_{\text{econ}} = P \cdot Q - C_{\text{explicit}} - C_{\text{implicit}}$$ (7.2)

8 Perfect Competition

8.2 How Perfectly Competitive Firms Make Output Decisions

A perfectly competitive firm has only one major decision to make—namely, what quantity to produce. To understand this, consider a quadratic short-run total cost function with fixed cost $F$ and convex variable cost:

$$TC(q) = F + c_1 q + \frac{1}{2} c_2 q^2$$ (8.1)

Since a perfectly competitive firm is a price taker, it faces a horizontal demand curve at the market price $P$. Its profit maximization problem is:

$$\max_{q \ge 0} \Pi(q) = P \cdot q - \left(F + c_1 q + \frac{1}{2} c_2 q^2\right)$$ (8.2)

Taking the first-order condition with respect to output $q$ equates marginal revenue ($MR = P$) with marginal cost ($MC(q) = c_1 + c_2 q$):

$$P = MC(q) = c_1 + c_2 q \implies q^* = \frac{P - c_1}{c_2}$$ (8.3)

Worked Example 8.1: Output and Profit at a Raspberry Farm
Suppose market price is $P = 4$, fixed cost is $F = 2$, linear unit cost is $c_1 = 0$, and quadratic cost slope is $c_2 = 1$. Then optimal output is $q^* = 4.0$, total revenue is $TR = 16.0$, total cost is $TC = 10.0$, and maximum profit is $\Pi^* = 6.0$.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0.0 | 2.0 | 0.0 | 2.0 | 0.0 | -2.0 |
| 1 | 4.0 | 2.0 | 0.5 | 2.5 | 1.0 | 1.5 |
| 2 | 8.0 | 2.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 | 2.0 | 4.0 |
| 3 | 12.0 | 2.0 | 4.5 | 6.5 | 3.0 | 5.5 |

8.3 Entry and Exit Decisions in the Long Run

In the long run, positive economic profits attract entry, driving the market price down until price equals the minimum of long-run average cost:

$$P_{LR} = \min_{q > 0} AC(q) = c_1 + \sqrt{2 F c_2}$$ (8.4)
